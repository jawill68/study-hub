What Goes Wrong, Why It Goes Wrong, and How to Fix It

The Question Bank shows what good answers look like. This resource shows what weak answers look like — and exactly why they fail. Eighteen examples diagnose the most common General Paper errors across six categories, with the specific weak attempt, the specific diagnosis, and the specific fix for each. Students often learn more from seeing mistakes named than from seeing model answers.

How This Resource Works

Diagnostic, not exhaustive. The 18 examples in this Autopsy cover the most common General Paper mistakes — the ones Cambridge examiners see in script after script. Mastering these patterns will lift student writing across most question types.

Each example contains four parts:

1. The question prompt — the actual task being attempted.
2. The weak student attempt — an excerpt showing the specific error in action.
3. The diagnosis — what specifically went wrong and why it costs marks.
4. The stronger version — the same content rewritten without the error.
5. The key takeaway — a one-line lesson to remember.

The six categories

Command Word Errors — misreading what the question actually asks. Argument Structure Errors — weak thesis, no counterargument, opinion in place of argument. Evidence Errors — anecdotal, fabricated, or misused evidence. Analysis Depth Errors — description instead of analysis, surface engagement. Evaluation Errors — one-sided, false dichotomies, vague nuance. Style and Register Errors — wrong tone, vague language, sloppy execution.
