Recommended Study Path
1
Read the Course Guide first
Understand the three Assessment Objectives, the topic areas, and the command-word decoder before diving into content — this shapes how you should read everything else here.
2
Build vocabulary with the Essential 50
Work through the 50 highest-leverage terms first — these are the words examiners expect precise use of in nearly every essay. Star the ones you keep forgetting.
3
Sort out the pairs you keep mixing up
Once a few confusions show up in your writing or practice — correlation/causation, tone/register — look them up here for the side-by-side fix before they become a habit.
4
Practice under real conditions, Pass 1
Attempt questions timed, then compare against the model answer and mark scheme. Read every "common errors" section, even on questions you think you did well.
5
Diagnose your own weak points
Match what you're getting wrong against the 18 worked examples here. Recognising these patterns on sight is most of what separates a Level 3 from a Level 5 essay.
6
Self-grade a real answer
Write a full response and run it through the Self-Grader's rubric for an honest band estimate — and follow its feedback links straight back to the Autopsy or Confusions entry that explains your weakness.
7
Round out vocabulary with the Complete Reference
Once the Essential 50 feels solid, use the 100 topic-specific terms here as a lookup whenever an essay calls for precise vocabulary in a specific subject area.
8
Finish with the harder Pass 2 questions
These prompts are more demanding — they reward examining the question itself, not just answering it. A strong finish here is a good sign you're ready.

About This Hub

This page ties together the full General Paper resource set. Most tools here are reference and practice material — the Essential 50 Glossary is the only resource that saves your progress between visits (starred terms, stored on this device). The Self-Grader is designed as the series' cross-referencing center: every piece of feedback it gives points to a specific numbered entry in the Mistakes Autopsy or Common Confusions, so a diagnosis always leads somewhere actionable.
