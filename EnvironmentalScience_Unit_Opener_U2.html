📚 Biodiversity

Investigate the variety of life at all levels—genetic, species, and ecosystem. Biodiversity is the measure of life's complexity and forms the foundation of ecological stability. Understanding what drives biodiversity loss and what supports it is critical for conservation.

A rainforest with 300 tree species is more biodiverse than a planted monoculture of 1 tree species covering 10 times the area. Genetic diversity within a species determines its ability to adapt to disease and environmental change. This unit explores biodiversity at every scale and connects genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity to ecosystem function and human survival.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish between genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity and explain their interdependence
  • Analyze why invasive species outcompete native species and predict their impact
  • Evaluate the role of keystone species in maintaining ecosystem structure
  • Explain how genetic bottlenecks reduce biodiversity and adaptive capacity
  • Design a biodiversity monitoring strategy for a threatened ecosystem
